On odd perfect numbers with exactly one even exponent greater than 2  Pascal Ochem and Joshua Zelinsky 2026  Abstract  We show that if all the even exponents of an odd perfect number   N  are equal to 2 except for one, then 3 23 , 000 , 000 , 000   divides   N   .  1   Introduction  In this paper, we consider that   N   is an odd perfect number, that is,   N   is odd and   σ ( N   ) = 2 N   , where   σ   is the positive-divisor-sum function.   Euler proved that   N   =   p e   ·   m 2   where   p   is prime,   p   ≡   e   ≡   1 (mod 4) and   p   does not divide   m . We say that   p ,   e , and   p e   are respectively the special prime, the special exponent, and the special component. McDaniel [6] has shown that   m   cannot be square- free, i.e., at least one of the even exponents of   N   is at least 4.   We say that  N   is   restrained   if   N   has exactly one such exponent. If   N   is restrained, let   n q  denote the unique component such that   n ̸   =   p   and   q   ⩾   4. We say that   n ,   q , and  n q   are respectively the notable prime, the notable exponent, and the notable component. Finally, we say that a prime or a component of   N   is   ordinary   if its exponent is 2. Thus, the only non-ordinary primes are   p   and   n . Our aim is to get a lower bound on   q .
